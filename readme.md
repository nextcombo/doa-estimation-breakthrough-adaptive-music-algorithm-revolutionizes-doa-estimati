# DOA Estimation Breakthrough: Adaptive MUSIC Algorithm Revolutionizes DOA Estimation with Micro-Differentiation

The direction of arrival (DoA) estimation is a common perceptual issue in radar, sonar, audio, and wireless communication systems. With the rise of integrated perception and communication paradigms, its importance has become increasingly prominent. To fully leverage the potential of such perception systems, it is essential to consider the impact of hardware damage on performance. This study proposes a model-based joint DoA estimation and hardware damage learning scheme, specifically deriving the differentiable version of the Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm for efficient learning of the considered damage. This method supports both supervised and unsupervised learning strategies with practical application potential. Simulation results show that the proposed approach can successfully learn significant errors in antenna position and complex gain, and outperforms traditional MUSIC algorithms in DoA estimation tasks.
Introduction DoA estimation refers to the angle estimation of the wavefront incident on a sensor array. In modern wireless communication systems, DoA is widely used for tasks such as positioning, target tracking, beamforming, and interference management [1,2]. As interest in integrated perception and communication (ISAC) grows—that is, perception and communication are performed simultaneously—there is an increasing demand for efficient DoA estimation. Traditionally, various model-based algorithms have been proposed for DoA estimation problems [3-7]. Although subspace methods (such as Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC) [3] or Rotating Invariant Techniques for Signal Parameter Estimation (ESPRIT) [6]) can enhance estimation performance, these methods are sensitive to hardware damage. Several studies have evaluated the impact of hardware damage on wireless communication performance [8-12], including its influence on perception performance [13-15].
Machine learning has become an alternative to traditional signal processing methods in recent years, being applied across multiple domains of wireless communication, including beamforming [18], channel estimation [19], positioning [20], and decoding [21]. However, such machine learning methods can be considered "black boxes," with very low interpretability for the learned systems. In contrast, the model-based machine learning paradigm [22,23] enhances the interpretability of the learned structures by leveraging signal processing models to construct and initialize neural architectures. This paradigm has been applied to various communication problems, such as channel estimation [24,25], precoding [26], beam prediction [27], detection [28], and ISAC design [29,30].
The contribution of this paper lies in the application of the model-based machine learning paradigm to joint DoA estimation and hardware damage learning problems. The study explores the application of the differentiable MUSIC (DiffMUSIC) algorithm in estimating antenna array location errors and complex gain damage learning. By replacing the non-differentiable argmax step in the traditional MUSIC algorithm with a convex combination of DoA, a differentiable version is obtained. Additionally, both supervised and unsupervised learning strategies are proposed. Experiments based on synthetic data were conducted against various baseline methods, validating the effectiveness of the proposed method, demonstrating its ability to compensate for significant antenna array damage, specifically considering the position and complex gain errors of the individual radiation units constituting the antenna array.
Related work, there have been studies on the application of machine learning methods in DoA estimation (see [31-35] for a non-exhaustive list). Additionally, the model-based machine learning paradigm has also been applied to this task [36,37]. The MUSIC method assumes several system models, such as that the source must be uncorrelated and the signal must be narrowband. In [36,37], the authors aim to relax these assumptions by introducing SubspaceNet (a convolutional neural network (CNN) output substituted for the covariance matrix, which can be used in the MUSIC method). This method has been shown to be robust against effects of correlated sources and hardware damage. The proposed DiffMUSIC method in this paper aims to achieve hardware damage learning without using CNNs, with the advantage of significantly reducing the number of learnable parameters.
Article illustration
Conclusion: This paper explores the application of the differentiable version of the MUSIC algorithm in DoA estimation for learning hardware damage. By replacing the non-differentiable argmax search with a softmax-based method, a differentiable MUSIC algorithm is obtained, where each estimated DoA can be seen as a convex combination of DoAs. Additionally, supervised and unsupervised loss functions are proposed for this damage learning task.
The proposed method demonstrates its good hardware damage learning capability and DoA estimation performance through comparison with various baseline methods. Specifically, it shows that the proposed method can learn hardware damage even under high perceptual noise and low number of measurement snapshots. Moreover, the unsupervised learning strategy holds special significance as it can compensate for the hardware damage of an antenna array without labeled DoA, and this method can be extended to an online learning strategy to enable real-time learning of its own hardware damage by the antenna array.
Future work will focus on extending the differentiable MUSIC method to scenarios with correlated or partially correlated sources and applying it to near-field positioning.
